4Monitoring and safety systems have significant limitationsEstablished · High confidence

Assessment

Assessment

Evidence has moved beyond isolated assistance: frontier labs report large and rapidly increasing AI participation in real AI R&D workflows, higher experiment throughput and delegation of increasingly complex tasks, while independent evaluation expects noticeable researcher acceleration. But a large causal acceleration of the overall frontier research process has not yet been cleanly demonstrated.

6Recursive improvement becomes a sustained accelerating feedback loopEmerging · Moderate confidence

Assessment

But sustained acceleration — where AI-enabled improvements systematically make subsequent improvement faster or more effective — has not yet been demonstrated.

SEPARATE INSTRUMENT

AI Governance & Regulatory-Moat Tracker

Technical risk and our capacity to govern it are different questions. This tracker will assess proposals against demonstrated risk, coverage, compliance cost, startup and open-weight effects, incumbent advantage, evidence of capture or motive, and the strongest counterargument.

METHOD

Classification, not conviction

The ladder does not attempt to predict whether advanced AI will ultimately be safe or catastrophic. It breaks one commonly proposed pathway to catastrophic AI risk into a sequence of increasingly strong propositions and asks a narrower question: what does the available evidence currently justify us saying at each step?

This distinction matters. Evidence that an AI agent sometimes circumvents a control supports that proposition. It does not automatically establish that humans will permanently lose control of AI, still less that AI will cause human extinction.

How each rung is assessed

For each proposition, the review looks for the strongest available evidence both supporting and limiting the claim. Relevant evidence can include observed deployments and incidents, controlled experiments, independent evaluations, peer-reviewed research, and sufficiently detailed research from frontier laboratories. The further a claim moves from observed behaviour toward extrapolation about future systems, the more cautiously that evidence is treated.

Established: direct and substantial empirical evidence supports the proposition today. Emerging: credible evidence supports important parts of the proposition or its underlying mechanism, but the full claim has not yet been demonstrated. Speculative: the proposition remains primarily hypothetical, theoretical, or dependent on extrapolation beyond the available empirical evidence.

Confidence means confidence in the assessment

The confidence label describes confidence that the classification is appropriate; it is not a probability that the proposition will occur. For example, Speculative · High confidence means high confidence that the available evidence currently warrants a Speculative classification — not high confidence that the outcome itself will happen.

Evidence does not automatically propagate up the ladder

Evidence supporting a lower rung can make a higher rung more plausible, but it does not establish it. Demonstrating that AI can assist AI research does not demonstrate an intelligence explosion. Demonstrating strategic deception does not demonstrate persistent incompatible objectives. Demonstrating failures of monitoring does not demonstrate permanent loss of human control.

Every rung therefore carries its own evidentiary burden. At each transition the framework asks: what evidence supports this particular proposition?

Observation and extrapolation are kept separate

Observed real-world behaviour generally carries more evidentiary weight than hypothetical scenarios. Controlled evaluations can provide powerful evidence about capabilities and tendencies, but their external validity matters: a model behaving badly in a deliberately constructed scenario is not necessarily evidence that it possesses a persistent real-world objective.

Expert forecasts and theoretical arguments can identify important risks and mechanisms, but they are not treated as equivalent to empirical demonstrations. The aim is not to dismiss theoretical risk; it is to keep visible what has been observed, what has been inferred, and what remains hypothesised.

Limitations count as evidence too

Each full evidence record includes limitations and countervailing evidence as well as evidence supporting the proposition. A result may demonstrate a capability while simultaneously revealing important constraints. An agent might circumvent one control but fail at long-horizon planning; a self-improving research system might improve its own scaffolding while leaving the underlying foundation model unchanged. Those qualifications are part of the assessment, not exceptions to it.

Every classification has a change test

The assessment does not merely state what the evidence supports today. Each full record specifies the kind of evidence that would justify changing its classification. New evidence is therefore tested against an explicit threshold rather than simply absorbed into a pre-existing narrative.

Movement can be upward or downward. Stronger evidence may move a proposition from Speculative to Emerging or from Emerging to Established. Conversely, better evaluations may reveal that an apparent capability depended on unrealistic conditions, prompting artefacts or weak controls, weakening the classification.

Move the classification when the evidence moves.

The ladder is not a probability model

The framework does not assign a probability to AI extinction, multiply conditional probabilities between rungs, or assume that one rung inevitably leads to the next. It is better understood as an evidence map of a proposed causal pathway: a way to show where empirical support is strong, where it becomes incomplete, and where the argument currently crosses from demonstrated phenomena into extrapolation.

The objective is not to prove that AI catastrophe will happen or that it cannot happen. It is to make the boundary between evidence and extrapolation visible — and to move that boundary when the evidence changes.

Evidence first. Conclusions provisional.
